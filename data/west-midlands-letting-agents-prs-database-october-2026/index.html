How many West Midlands letting agents' websites give the PRS Database start date? October 2026

We read the public websites of 109 letting agents in the West Midlands on 4 October 2026. 5 named the "Register your rental property" service or gave the 15 December 2026 start. 26 more mentioned a national landlord database in general terms. On 78 we found no mention.

A convenience sample, not a census. Counts only: no agent is named. This is what we found on the websites, not what the agents know or do.

What we found

We counted one thing: whether an agent's public website names England's "Register your rental property" service or gives its start date in the West Midlands, 15 December 2026. A website is only one of the ways an agent talks to landlords.

5 of 109

Service named or start date given

On 5 of the 109 websites we read, a page names the "Register your rental property" service or gives 15 December 2026, December 2026 or the 14 March 2027 deadline for the national database.

2 of the 5 are firms we already knew had written about it. They were on our own list for that reason.

26 of 109

General mention only

26 more websites mention a national landlord database or register in general terms, with no date and no service name. None of the pages we read on these sites gives the start date.

78 of 109

No mention found

On 78 of the 109 we found no mention on the pages we read. There may be a page we did not read.

5

Websites we could not read

We tried 114 websites. 5 could not be read, for example because the site asked for a browser check or could not be reached safely. They are left out of the count.

National brands and other firms

15 of the sites belong to national or multi-region brands. Their pages are written centrally, for the whole brand or by an office in another part of the country. 3 of these 15 sites named the service or gave the start date. On all 3 the page we found was written for the whole country or for landlords in another region, not for the West Midlands in particular.

The other 99 sites are West Midlands firms with one office or several. We read 94 of them. 2 named the service or gave the start date, and both are firms we already knew had written about it. On the other 92 we found no page that names the service or gives the date: 17 mention a national database in general terms and on 75 we found no mention.

Why the West Midlands, and why now

Under the draft regulations the West Midlands is the first of nine regions to start. The rule starts there on 15 December 2026. The Government's Housing Hub guidance gives 14 March 2027 as the deadline to register in the West Midlands. The regulations are still draft.

We read the websites on 4 October 2026, about ten weeks before the start date.

  • GOV.UK Housing Hub: Get ready, "Register your rental property" service

Method

  • Region. The official West Midlands region: Birmingham and the Black Country, Coventry, Solihull, Staffordshire, Stoke-on-Trent, Warwickshire, Worcestershire, Herefordshire, Shropshire, and Telford and Wrekin.
  • Sample. 60 letting agents we already held on our own list, plus 54 found by searching the web for "letting agents" and the name of each of 18 larger places. For each place we took the first three results that were an agent's own website and not already in the sample. We skipped property portals and directories. A chain's website was counted once.
  • First method: reading the site. A script read each site's robots.txt file first and kept to it. It then read the home page and up to eight more pages whose address or link text suggested news, a blog, landlord advice or legislation. On WordPress sites it also used the site's own search. It made at most 12 requests to a site, at least one second apart.
  • Second method: a search engine. For each of the 105 sites where the script found no dated page, we ran two searches limited to that site, one for "15 December 2026" and one for "register your rental property". Where a result looked like an article about the register we read that page. This changed 1 site, which moved into the dated count.
  • The three groups. Service named or start date given: a page we read names the "Register your rental property" service, or gives 15 December 2026, December 2026 or 14 March 2027 for the national database. General mention only: a page speaks of the national landlord database or register with no date and no service name. No mention found: neither. We read every sentence that put a site in the first group. Other schemes do not count: selective or HMO licensing, Rent Smart Wales and the Scottish landlord register.
  • A test of the method. We ran the same script on 5 West Midlands agents outside the sample that a web search showed had a dated article. 1 could not be read because its site asked for a browser check. On each of the other 4 the script found the dated page. The search engine check found the dated page for all 5. These 5 agents are not in the count.
  • Hand checks. We opened 10 sites from the "other firms" group that were not in the dated count, picked at random, and looked at their news or blog pages for posts from 9 September 2026 onwards, using the site's own search where there was one. We found no dated page on any of them. On 1 of the 10 we found a general mention the script had missed, and we moved that site and 2 more sites that link to the same page into the general group. We also checked 5 sites from the first two groups and all were in the right group. An earlier hand check of 10 sites found 1 missed mention. 2 more sites were moved into the general group after a separate check of two large sites, and 2 after a wider re-read of the pages already read. The search check and the hand checks opened up to three more pages on a site.
  • Could not be read. We count a site as not read when its robots.txt does not allow reading or asks robots to wait minutes between pages, when the site turns the request away or asks for a browser check, when it cannot be reached safely, when it has moved to another address, or when its pages show no text without JavaScript. We did not try to get round any of these. These sites are left out of the count.

Limits

  • This is a convenience sample, not a random one and not a census. It does not tell you the share of all West Midlands letting agents.
  • A website is not the only way an agent talks to landlords. Agents also use email, letters and phone calls. We did not see any of those.
  • Pages we could not read are left out.
  • Our search can miss a page. The script read between 1 and 10 pages on a site (the middle site had 5), not the whole site. The search engine check only finds pages the search engine has indexed. A site may give the date on a page neither method found.
  • The split between a general mention and no mention is less sure than the dated count. Large sites that share pages across brands are where we found missed general mentions.
  • 2 of the 5 sites in the dated count were on our own list because they had already written about the database. Without them the dated count is 3 of 107.
  • Pages on national brands' sites are written centrally. A page that gives the date may be written for another region.
  • It is one day's snapshot. A site may have changed since.
  • There is no trend. We have not counted before.
