What the PRS Database will ask you for

Every private landlord in England will have to register each let property on the new Private Rented Sector Database ("Register your rental property"). The draft regulations, laid before Parliament on 9 September 2026, set out the timetable and the information required.

  • Licence type and licence numbers, bedrooms, occupants and households.
  • Gas safety record and its issue date; EICR and its expiry date; EPC or exemption.
  • Rent, payment frequency, whether bills are included, and furnishing.
  • Freeholder, superior landlord and property manager contact details.
  • Changes must be updated within 28 days, and entries renewed every year.

The Readiness sheet has one row per property. Pick the region and it shows your opening date and deadline, counts what is ready and what is outstanding, and gives you the 28-day update date whenever something changes.

These regulations are still in draft and subject to Parliamentary approval, so dates and details may change. Sources: GOV.UK Housing Hub, Get ready: 'Register your rental property' service, and the draft Private Rented Sector Database Regulations 2026.

Why this edition

The private rented sector in England changed on 1 May 2026. A tracker written before then is tracking the wrong things.

  • Section 21 "no fault" evictions were abolished and tenancies became periodic.
  • Rent can go up once a year, using the section 13 procedure with at least two months' notice.
  • Maximum civil penalties for certain housing offences rose from £30,000 to £40,000.
  • Registration on the Private Rented Sector Database is due to open region by region from 15 December 2026, under regulations that are still in draft.
  • New Right to Rent guidance and a new code of practice apply to checks made from 1 October 2026; digital checks must use a registered provider (GOV.UK).

Source: GOV.UK, Implementing the Renters' Rights Act 2025: our roadmap.
