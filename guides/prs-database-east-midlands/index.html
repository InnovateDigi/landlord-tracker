PRS Database in the East Midlands: dates and how to get ready

The rule starts in the East Midlands on 15 February 2027. Here is the timetable, which councils are included, and a step-by-step checklist to get ready.

Facts last checked 3 October 2026 against GOV.UK and legislation.gov.uk.

What is still draft

  • The rules in this guide come from the draft Private Rented Sector Database Regulations 2026. They were laid for approval by both Houses of Parliament. legislation.gov.uk says they have not yet been made as a UK Statutory Instrument.
  • The Act sections that create the registration duty and the penalties are not yet in force. The Government's explanatory memorandum says Commencement No. 4 Regulations will bring the database provisions into force. We could not find those regulations on legislation.gov.uk on 3 October 2026.
  • The £65 fee comes from Government guidance. The draft regulations leave the amount to the database operator, so it could change.
  • The start dates are in the draft regulations. The register-by deadlines come from Government guidance on the Housing Hub.
  • Dates and details could change before the regulations are made. Check GOV.UK before you act.

"Rule starts" is the date the draft regulations come into force for properties in the East Midlands. It is the date from which you are legally required to register. The 14 May 2027 deadline comes from Government guidance. The draft regulations give the start date only.

Which councils are in the East Midlands?

The draft regulations define the East Midlands by these councils. The list uses the councils as they existed on 15 December 2026.

  • Derby City Council
  • Derbyshire County Council
  • Leicester City Council
  • Leicestershire County Council
  • Lincolnshire County Council
  • North Northamptonshire Council
  • Nottingham City Council
  • Nottinghamshire County Council
  • Rutland County Council
  • West Northamptonshire Council

Your property's location sets the deadline, not where you live. A property in the East Midlands follows these dates, wherever its landlord lives.

Many parts of England have two tiers of local government. A property can have both a county council and a district, borough or city council. County councils are responsible for services across the whole of a county.

To check a property, enter its postcode in GOV.UK's Find your local council tool. The tool can show more than one council. For example, it shows Rugby Borough Council and Warwickshire County Council for the postcode CV21 2LS. Look for any of the names it shows in the list above.

You can also type a council's name into Find your region. Other regions? See PRS Database dates by region.

Step-by-step preparation checklist

  1. Confirm the property is in the East Midlands. Check its council against the list above.
  2. Decide which properties to register now. Let properties need registering. Empty properties can wait for further guidance.
  3. Check each tenancy is in scope. Assured and regulated tenancies must be registered. Supported exempt accommodation is excluded.
  4. Set up a GOV.UK One Login. The service needs one.
  5. Collect your own details as landlord. Individuals give name, date of birth, residential address, telephone number and email address. Companies and other organisations give more.
  6. Collect each property's details. Address, ownership type, dwelling type, bedrooms, whether it is let, and the freeholder, any superior landlord and any property manager.
  7. Gather the tenancy details. Occupants, households, licence numbers, rent, whether bills are included, and furnishing.
  8. Find your certificates and check the dates. Gas safety record, EICR or EIC, and EPC.
  9. If you use an agent, agree who supplies what. You still start the registration yourself.
  10. Register and pay the fee. You can register from 15 December 2026. Your region's rule starts 15 February 2027. Aim to finish well before 14 May 2027. The fee is £65 per property per year.
  11. Diary your reminders. Update within 28 days of any change, and renew every 12 months.

Questions

When does PRS Database registration open in the East Midlands?

The draft regulations start the rule for the East Midlands on 15 February 2027. That is the date from which you are legally required to register. Government guidance says you can register from 15 December 2026, in any region.

What is the PRS Database deadline for the East Midlands?

Government guidance gives 14 May 2027 as the deadline to register properties in the East Midlands. That is three months after the start date.

Which areas count as the East Midlands for the PRS Database?

The draft regulations define the East Midlands by 10 named councils. They include Derby City Council, Derbyshire County Council and Leicester City Council. The full list is on this page.

I live outside the East Midlands but let a property there. Which deadline applies?

The property's location decides it, not where you live. Government guidance gives an example. If you live in London but your rental property is in Birmingham, you must meet the West Midlands deadline. So a property in the East Midlands follows the East Midlands dates.

Do I have to register an empty property in the East Midlands?

Not yet. Government guidance says you only need to register properties that are let, or become let during the rollout. Wait for further guidance on empty properties.
