Guide for landlords in England

The PRS Database: what landlords in England need to do

England is creating a register of private landlords and their rental properties. This guide explains who must register, when, what it costs and what you will be asked for.

Facts last checked 3 October 2026 against GOV.UK and legislation.gov.uk.

What is still draft

  • The rules in this guide come from the draft Private Rented Sector Database Regulations 2026. They were laid for approval by both Houses of Parliament. legislation.gov.uk says they have not yet been made as a UK Statutory Instrument.
  • The Act sections that create the registration duty and the penalties are not yet in force. The Government's explanatory memorandum says Commencement No. 4 Regulations will bring the database provisions into force. We could not find those regulations on legislation.gov.uk on 3 October 2026.
  • The £65 fee comes from Government guidance. The draft regulations leave the amount to the database operator, so it could change.
  • The start dates are in the draft regulations. The register-by deadlines come from Government guidance on the Housing Hub.
  • Government guidance for letting agents and property managers is still to be published.
  • The list of information the public will be able to see is still to be published.
  • Dates and details could change before the regulations are made. Check GOV.UK before you act.

The short version

  • Landlords of assured or regulated tenancies in England must register themselves and each property.
  • Registration rolls out region by region from 15 December 2026, starting in the West Midlands. Each region then has three months.
  • Everyone actively letting property needs to be registered by 14 November 2027.
  • The fee is £65 per property per year.
  • If you do not register, you risk a fine.

What is the PRS Database?

The Private Rented Sector (PRS) Database is a national register created by the Renters' Rights Act 2025. The Government's name for the service is "Register your rental property".

It holds two kinds of entry. A landlord entry is about you. A dwelling entry is about one rented property.

The Government says the aim is to help councils enforce the rules, give tenants more information, and encourage compliance with existing health and safety standards.

In future, tenants will be able to use the service to see whether a landlord is compliant with key legal requirements.

Who must register?

Government guidance says that if you are a landlord of an assured or regulated tenancy, you must register yourself and each of your properties.

  • The Act defines a residential landlord as the landlord under an assured or regulated tenancy of a dwelling in England that is not social housing.
  • Landlords of supported exempt accommodation do not need to register.
  • Joint landlords each make their own landlord entry but share one dwelling entry.
  • The rules cover rental properties in England.
  • Where you live does not matter. The property's location sets your deadline.
  • For now, you only need to register properties that are let, or that become let during the rollout.
  • Later, you will also need to register an empty property before you or your agent markets it for let.

Not sure whether your tenancy counts as assured? That depends on the Housing Act 1988. Ask your council or take legal advice.

When must I register?

Each region has its own start date. Once your region's rule starts, you have three months to register.

"Rule starts" is the date the draft regulations come into force for properties in that region. It is the date from which you are legally required to register. The register-by deadlines come from Government guidance.

  • You can register early. Government guidance says you can register whenever you want from 15 December 2026, but before your region's deadline.
  • After a region's deadline, councils in that region can start enforcement.

What does it cost?

  • Government guidance gives an annual fee of £65 per property. You renew it each year.
  • The fee is for one property. You pay it for each property you register.
  • During the rollout, the fee is pro-rated so that people who sign up first do not pay more.
  • Under the draft regulations, the fee must be paid before a dwelling entry is made, and again to renew it.
  • The draft regulations do not set the amount. The database operator decides it, so it could change.
  • Letting an entry lapse can cost more. The Act allows a higher fee to make a lapsed entry active again.

What information will I be asked for?

Here is a summary. The printable checklist has every item.

About you, if you are an individual

Name, date of birth, residential address, telephone number and email address.

If you are a company or other organisation

Name, type of legal entity, address (not a PO box) and contact details. Who is making the entry and a nominated contact. Your Companies House or charity number if you have one.

About each property

Address, ownership type, dwelling type, bedrooms and whether it is currently let. Your correspondence address and email. The name and email of the freeholder, any superior landlord and any property manager.

About the tenancy

Number of occupants and households. Whether it needs an HMO, additional or selective licence, and the licence numbers. Rent and how often it is paid. Whether bills are included. Whether it is furnished.

Safety and energy documents

A copy of the gas safety record (if there is gas) and its issue date. A copy of the EICR or EIC and its expiry or issue date. Whether an EPC was required, and a copy of the latest one.

How you register

You need a GOV.UK One Login. Offline routes will be available for people who need them. Entries are made on the operator's website or on its form. A contact centre will help once the service is live.

Keeping your entry up to date

  • If a property is already let, the draft regulations let you make the dwelling entry first. The tenancy and safety information must follow within 28 days, or the entry becomes inactive. Draft
  • When a new tenancy starts in a property that was empty, add the tenancy information within 28 days. Draft
  • If anything in your entry goes out of date, update it within 28 days. Draft
  • The draft regulations do not define "out of date". Updating whenever your details change is the safe approach.
  • Entries last 12 months. Renew by the renewal date or the entry becomes inactive. Draft
  • If you register two or more properties, the Government says the renewal dates will be synchronised.
  • If you make a landlord entry but no dwelling entry within three months, the landlord entry becomes inactive.

What happens if I do not register?

  • Government guidance says you risk being fined. The Government's announcement calls registration a legal requirement.
  • After a region's deadline, councils in that region can start enforcement.
  • The Act sets maximum penalties. A council can impose a financial penalty of up to £7,000 for breaking the registration rules.
  • The maximum is £40,000 for offences. These include knowingly or recklessly giving false or misleading information, carrying on after a penalty, and breaking the rules again within five years.
  • The council decides the amount, up to those maximums.
  • A court may not make a possession order while a landlord is in breach of the duty to have active entries. There are two exceptions, Grounds 7A and 14.
  • Nobody may market a property for let without active landlord and dwelling entries. The Government says this applies later, to empty properties.

These penalties come from Act sections that are not yet in force. We have not confirmed when they start.

What to do now

  1. Find your region and date. Use dates by region.
  2. Set up a GOV.UK One Login. The service needs one.
  3. Gather your details and certificates. Use the printable checklist.
  4. If you use an agent, agree who supplies what. You still start the registration yourself.
  5. Diary your reminders. Update within 28 days of any change, and renew every 12 months.

Questions

What is the PRS Database?

It is a national register of private landlords and rental properties in England, created by the Renters' Rights Act 2025. The Government's name for the service is "Register your rental property". It holds landlord entries and dwelling entries.

Who has to register on the PRS Database?

Government guidance says landlords of assured or regulated tenancies must register themselves and each of their properties. Landlords of supported exempt accommodation do not need to.

When does PRS Database registration open?

The Government says the service rolls out from 15 December 2026, starting in the West Midlands, then region by region. Each region then has three months to register. Everyone actively letting property needs to be registered by 14 November 2027.

How much does it cost to register?

Government guidance gives an annual fee of £65 per property, renewed each year. The draft regulations leave the amount to the database operator, so it could change.

What happens if I do not register?

Government guidance says you risk being fined. After a region's deadline, councils there can start enforcement. The Act sets maximum penalties of £7,000 and £40,000, but the sections that create them are not yet in force.

Do I still have to register if I use a letting agent?

Yes. You still need to start the registration yourself. Your agent may be able to upload certain information for you, and you stay responsible for it.

Do I need to register an empty property?

Not yet. For now you only need to register properties that are let, or that become let during the rollout. Later, empty properties will need registering before you market them.
